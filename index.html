When Price Leaves NAV Behind: The Dangerous 18% Premium in India’s Global Tech ETFs

By Analytical Bureau · Focus: SEBI $7 Billion Overseas Cap & Retail Arbitrage Trap

An escalating structural distortion has taken root across the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). Due to the decade-old $7-billion regulatory industry limit imposed by the Reserve Bank of India on domestic mutual funds investing abroad, Indian asset managers have been legally barred from issuing new ETF creation units in overseas tech strategies for months.

The consequence? When domestic retail investors rush to buy US mega-cap technology and semiconductor shares via local demat accounts, Authorised Participants (APs) cannot create fresh ETF units to arbitrage the price down to fair value. The ETFs have effectively transformed into closed-end funds trading under severe structural rationing.

The Regulatory Reckoning

SEBI has reportedly written to North Block and the RBI requesting a phased recalibration of the overseas ceiling to accommodate the ₹75,000 Crore of domestic wealth seeking global geographic diversification.

Until the RBI yields, retail buyers purchasing MAFANG or MON100 at these market levels are effectively buying a derivative at an 18% surcharge above fair liquidation value. When the policy gate eventually unlocks, the price crash toward actual NAV will be sudden and unhedged.
